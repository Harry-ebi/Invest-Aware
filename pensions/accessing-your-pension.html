Accessing a pension

An introduction to the vocabulary of taking money from a pension. This teaches the terms — it isn't retirement advice.

WhenThe optionsTax

Minimum pension access age

Pensions are designed for later life, so there's normally a minimum age before you can access the money. It's currently 55 for most people, and is due to rise to 57 from April 2028. (This is separate from the State Pension age, which is different again and also changes over time.)

Tax-free cash

When accessing a pension, many people can typically take a portion as a tax-free lump sum, with the rest treated as taxable income when drawn. The specifics — including limits — depend on the rules in force and your circumstances.

The main ways to take an income

Drawdown

You keep the pension invested and draw money from it flexibly over time. The pot can keep growing or falling, and could run down if you withdraw more than it sustains.

Annuity

You exchange some or all of the pot for a guaranteed income, often for life. This trades flexibility and growth potential for certainty of income.

These aren't the only options, and they can sometimes be combined. Each has trade-offs around flexibility, certainty, investment risk and what happens to any remaining money.

Tax on withdrawals

Beyond any tax-free portion, pension withdrawals are generally taxable as income. That means how and when you take money out can affect the tax you pay — one reason accessing a pension is an area where many people seek regulated advice.

Terminology, not advice

This page introduces the vocabulary so the options feel less intimidating. It is not retirement advice. Decisions about accessing a pension can be complex and hard to reverse, depend on personal circumstances, and involve rules that change. Guidance services and regulated advisers exist to help — and checking current GOV.UK information is a sensible first step.
