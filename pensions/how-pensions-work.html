How pensions work

Strip away the jargon and a pension is more familiar than it looks — it's a wrapper, like the others, with its own rules.

What it isWrapper, not investmentThe trade-off

A pension is essentially a long-term investment account with particular tax advantages and rules about when the money can be accessed.

That single sentence does a lot of work. A pension isn't a mysterious product — it's an account designed for the long term. Money goes in (often from several sources), it gets invested, and it can eventually be used to provide an income in later life.

A pension is not itself an investment

This is the most common source of confusion, and it's the same wrapper vs investment idea from the accounts section:

Say it clearly

A pension is the account. Investments sit inside it. So “how is my pension doing?” really means “how are the investments inside my pension doing?” — see how pensions are invested.

A pension's life, in three stages

However the details differ, most pensions follow the same broad arc:

Money goes inyour contributions, often an employer's, plus tax relief
It's invested and (hopefully) growsover years or decades, rising and falling along the way
It provides income lateraccessed from a minimum age, in retirement

The core trade-off

Pensions offer particular tax advantages and, in workplace schemes, employer contributions — genuinely valuable features. In return, the money is generally locked away until a minimum age. That restriction isn't just a downside: it's part of what makes a pension a long-term pot, which is exactly the setting in which compounding does its most striking work.

The main types

Workplace pensions

Arranged through your employer, usually with employer contributions and automatic enrolment.

Personal pensions & SIPPs

Pensions you set up yourself, often with a wider choice of investments.

There's also the State Pension, provided by the government based on your National Insurance record — separate from the workplace and personal pensions this section focuses on.

Not advice

Pensions involve rules that can change and decisions that depend on personal circumstances. InvestAware explains how they work; it doesn't tell you what to do with yours.
