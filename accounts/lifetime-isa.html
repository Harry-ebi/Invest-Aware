Lifetime ISA (LISA)

A wrapper with a government top-up attached — and some firm conditions to understand before using it.

A Lifetime ISA is a type of ISA designed to help with two specific goals: buying a first home, or saving for later life. Its headline feature is a government bonus on what you pay in — but that bonus comes with rules.

The main features

  • Eligibility. You generally need to be aged 18 to 39 to open one, and can typically keep contributing until age 50.
  • Annual contribution allowance. You can pay in up to £4,000 per tax year. This counts towards your overall ISA allowance of £20,000.
  • Government bonus. The government adds a 25% bonus on contributions — for example, £1,000 added by the government for every £4,000 you pay in.
  • First-home use. The money (including the bonus) can be put towards a first home, within the scheme's price and eligibility limits.
  • Later-life use. Alternatively it can be accessed from a set age for use later in life.

The withdrawal charge — the crucial catch

Read this carefully

If you withdraw money for a reason other than those the LISA is designed for (and outside the permitted circumstances), a withdrawal charge of 25% generally applies. Because of how the charge is calculated, it can take back more than the bonus you received — meaning you could get back less than you put in. This is why a LISA suits money genuinely earmarked for its intended purposes.

Cash vs Stocks & Shares LISA

Like other ISAs, a LISA comes in two flavours. A Cash LISA pays interest, more like saving. A Stocks & Shares LISA holds investments, more like a Stocks & Shares ISA — with the potential for growth and the risk of falls. Which fits depends partly on your time horizon.

No verdict from us

A LISA isn't inherently “better” or “worse” than an ISA or a pension — it's a different tool with different rules and trade-offs. How it compares for a particular person depends on their goals and circumstances. We don't tell you which to use.
