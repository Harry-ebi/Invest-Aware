About InvestAware

Straightforward, impartial financial education. No products to sell. No sales pitch.

Why we exist

InvestAware exists to make investing easier to understand. Financial products can be complicated, financial terminology can be confusing, and investing is often presented either as something only experts can grasp or as a way to get rich quickly. Neither is particularly helpful.

We provide straightforward educational information about investing, pensions and long-term financial planning — aimed at people who know they should probably understand this better, but don't know where to start.

What we are — and aren't

We don't sell investments.
We don't recommend providers.
We don't receive commission for promoting products.
We don't provide personalised financial advice.

Our aim is simply to help people understand their options and ask better questions.

That independence is the whole point. Because we have nothing to sell, we can explain the reasoning behind ideas rather than steering you towards a product. The content itself is what we offer.

How the big ideas reinforce each other

Across the site you'll meet the same handful of concepts from different directions. They aren't separate facts to memorise — they connect into a single way of thinking about long-term investing.

Diversificationown many investments rather than relying on a few
Costskeep track of how much return is lost to charges
Staying investedavoid relying on short-term market predictions
Timelet investments share in long-term growth
Compoundinglet previous returns generate further returns

That chain is the central educational thread running through InvestAware. Read the pages in any order you like — but this is where they all point.

A simple framework: ten questions

To round off the learning journey, here's a checklist. Before investing, it can help to be able to answer these — not because there's a right answer to each, but because thinking them through tends to lead to better decisions.

  1. What am I investing for?
  2. When might I need the money?
  3. How much uncertainty can I tolerate?
  4. What am I actually investing in?
  5. Am I diversified?
  6. What does it cost?
  7. What account or wrapper am I using?
  8. What taxes might apply?
  9. What would cause me to change my strategy?
  10. Do I actually understand what I own?
What this is — and isn't

This is an educational checklist to prompt your own thinking. It is not a recommendation engine or a suitability assessment, and it doesn't produce a “right answer”. It's simply a set of questions worth being able to answer.

The InvestAware mental model

If the site does its job, you should end up able to summarise what you've learned roughly like this:

I need cash for things I may need soon. Investing is for money I can leave for longer.

Investments rise and fall, and higher returns aren't guaranteed. I can own lots of companies rather than trying to guess which one will win.

The fund I own and the ISA, pension or GIA I hold it in are different things. Fees and tax matter because small differences can compound.

Markets will fall sometimes, and predicting exactly when they'll fall or recover is extremely difficult — so time in the market can matter more than trying to time it.

Compounding becomes increasingly powerful given years or decades to work. And investing can actually be relatively simple.

If you leave understanding that, InvestAware has done what it set out to do: leave you better informed — not encouraged to buy anything.
