Stocks & Shares ISA

Probably the most talked-about account in UK investing — and often the most misunderstood.

An ISA is the box. Your investments are what you put inside it.

“ISA” stands for Individual Savings Account. But an ISA isn't itself an investment — it's a wrapper: an account with a particular tax treatment. A Stocks & Shares ISA is one that can hold investments such as funds, shares, ETFs and bonds, rather than just cash.

How it works

The annual allowance

There's a limit on how much you can pay into ISAs each tax year. For the current tax year it's £20,000, across all the ISAs you hold (so a Stocks & Shares ISA and a Cash ISA share the same overall allowance). A tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April.

What can be held inside one

A Stocks & Shares ISA can typically hold a range of investments — for example funds, individual shares, investment trusts, ETFs and some bonds. Exactly what's available depends on the provider you use.

Tax treatment

The appeal of the wrapper is its tax treatment. Broadly, returns generated inside a Stocks & Shares ISA — growth in value and income such as dividends or interest — are generally sheltered from UK income tax and capital gains tax. That's why the wrapper matters, and it connects directly to compounding: money not lost to tax can remain invested and potentially generate further returns. We explore that on tax efficiency & compounding.

Access to your money

Unlike a pension, a Stocks & Shares ISA doesn't restrict access by age — you can generally sell investments and withdraw when you choose, subject to how quickly investments can be sold. Remember, though, that the value can fall as well as rise, so the amount available isn't fixed.

Wrapper vs investment — again

It's worth repeating, because it's the crux: the ISA is not the investment. Two people could each hold a Stocks & Shares ISA and own completely different things inside them — and their results would depend on those investments, not on the ISA label. The wrapper shapes the tax and access; the investments inside shape the returns and the risk.

Rules can change

ISA allowances and rules are set by the government and can change from year to year. Figures on this page reflect the tax year noted below and should be checked against current GOV.UK guidance before you rely on them. We don't recommend providers, and nothing here is personal advice.
