Shares

The most direct way to own a stake in a business — and a good place to understand where investment risk comes from.

Buying a share means owning a very small part of a company.

A company's ownership is divided into shares (also called equities or stocks). Buy one and you own a genuine, if tiny, slice of that business — with a stake in how it performs.

One highlighted slice among many — that's roughly the idea. Real companies are divided into millions of shares, so a single share is a very small stake indeed.

How shares can produce returns

There are two main ways a shareholder might benefit:

  • Share-price growth. If the company becomes more valuable, the price of its shares may rise, and you could sell for more than you paid. It may also fall.
  • Dividends. Some companies pay out part of their profits to shareholders as dividends. Not all companies do, and dividends can be reduced or stopped.

The risks are real

Owning part of a business means sharing in its fortunes — good and bad:

  • Share prices fluctuate, sometimes sharply and for reasons outside the company's control.
  • Companies can perform badly, and their shares can fall a long way.
  • Companies can fail entirely, in which case shares can become worthless.

Because of that last point, putting a lot of money into a single company's shares concentrates your outcome on one business. That's precisely the situation diversification is designed to reduce — and why so many investors hold shares through funds rather than one at a time.
