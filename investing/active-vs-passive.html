Active vs passive investing

Two genuinely different philosophies. Our aim here is to explain both fairly — not to crown a winner.

ActivePassiveThe honest trade-offs

At the simplest level: an active investment tries to do better than a market, while a passive investment tries to match one. Both are legitimate. Each has real advantages and real drawbacks.

Active

A manager chooses investments intending to outperform a benchmark, or to pursue another defined objective.

Potential advantages

  • Active decision-making and judgement
  • The potential to outperform a market
  • The ability to avoid particular investments
  • Can pursue goals an index doesn't capture

Potential disadvantages

  • Usually higher cost
  • Choosing the right manager matters — and is hard
  • Outperformance isn't guaranteed
  • Results can vary considerably

Passive

An investment attempts to track an index or market rather than beat it.

Potential advantages

  • Usually lower cost
  • Broad diversification can be easy to achieve
  • Simple and transparent
  • Doesn't require picking individual winners

Potential disadvantages

  • Designed to track, not beat, its index (before costs)
  • Still experiences market falls in full
  • Index construction decides what you own
  • Broad indexes can still contain concentrations

Why passive connects to “buy the haystack”

Broad passive funds have become a common way to implement diversification, and it's worth understanding why — without treating it as an endorsement. A broad market index lets an investor participate in the returns of many companies at once, without trying to predict which individual businesses will do best. Rather than asking “which technology company will dominate in twenty years?”, an investor could own many technology companies alongside firms from many other industries.

If tomorrow's winner is already inside a broad portfolio, the investor participates in its growth without having needed to identify it beforehand — the haystack idea in action.

Staying neutral

None of this makes passive universally better. Active investing has a genuine case, and many investors use both. Cost matters (see investment costs), but it isn't the only thing that matters. InvestAware doesn't recommend one approach over the other.
