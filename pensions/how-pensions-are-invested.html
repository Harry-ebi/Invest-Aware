How pensions are invested

The part many people never look at — and the part that quietly shapes the outcome.

Your pension is the account. Inside it are investments.

This is worth making very clear, because it changes how you think about a pension entirely. The money you and your employer pay in doesn't just sit there — it's used to buy investments, and it's those investments that grow (or fall) over time.

What can sit inside a pension

The investments held within a pension may include:

Shares· Bonds· Funds· Cash· Other assets

In many workplace pensions, members are placed into a default fund unless they choose otherwise, often with some alternative options. A SIPP may offer a wider range. To understand what these building blocks actually are, see funds, shares and bonds.

Why two identical contributions can grow differently

A telling example

Two people could pay exactly the same amount into a pension over the same period and still end up with different pots — because their money may be invested differently, in investments that performed differently. The contributions are only part of the story; what the money is invested in matters too.

This is also why the same forces from the rest of the site apply inside a pension: diversification, costs, risk and return, and — given the long time horizons involved — compounding. A pension is, in the end, long-term investing wearing a particular tax-and-access wrapper.

Not advice

How a pension should be invested depends on personal circumstances, including timeframe and attitude to risk. InvestAware explains how it works, not what you should choose. Default funds and options vary by scheme.
