Asset allocation

Diversification asks “how widely do I spread?” Asset allocation asks “across what types of thing?”

Combining different types of asset

Asset allocation is the mix of broad asset types in a portfolio. A common way to describe it is a blend of:

Shares+ Bonds+ Cash

Each of these behaves differently. Shares have historically offered higher long-term growth potential but with larger ups and downs. Bonds have tended to be steadier, though they carry their own risks. Cash is the most stable in nominal terms but is most exposed to inflation over time.

Why the combination matters

Because these assets don't all move together, mixing them changes the overall character of a portfolio — typically its balance between potential growth and the size of its swings. A portfolio tilted heavily towards shares may aim for more growth while accepting larger falls; one with more bonds and cash may be steadier but with less long-term growth potential. Neither is “correct” in the abstract — it depends on the investor's goals, timeframe and capacity for loss.

The mix of assets is one of the biggest influences on how a portfolio behaves — often more so than the choice of any single investment within it.

No prescribed percentages here

You'll sometimes see confident rules of thumb about exactly what percentage should be in shares or bonds at a given age. InvestAware deliberately doesn't prescribe percentages, because the “right” allocation depends on personal circumstances we can't know. The useful takeaway is the principle: different assets play different roles, and the blend shapes the outcome.

Not advice

This page explains a concept, not a recommendation. How to allocate a portfolio is a personal decision, and for many people a matter to consider carefully or take regulated advice on.
