ETFs

Exchange-Traded Funds — a familiar idea in a slightly different wrapper.

What it isWhat it can holdA common myth

An ETF is a fund that trades on an exchange, much like a share.

“ETF” stands for Exchange-Traded Fund. Like any fund, it holds a collection of underlying investments. The distinctive feature is in the name: it's bought and sold on a stock exchange throughout the trading day, at a live price — whereas some other funds are dealt once a day directly with the provider.

Some other fundsdealt once a day, at a single price, with the provider

This is a difference in how you buy and sell, not in what the fund is for.

What an ETF can hold

  • It holds underlying investments, just like other funds.
  • Those can be shares, bonds or other assets.
  • Many ETFs are used for passive investing — tracking an index.
  • But ETFs can also follow active or specialist strategies.

A common misconception

Don't assume

ETF does not automatically mean “passive index tracker.” While many ETFs do track broad indexes, others are actively managed or focus on narrow, specialist areas. The “ETF” label describes how it trades, not what strategy it follows. Always look at what a specific ETF actually holds and how it's run.

So an ETF is best thought of as a structure — a way of packaging a fund so it trades on an exchange — rather than a particular investment style. The important questions are the same as for any fund: what does it hold, what is it trying to do, and what does it cost?
