Pensions

A pension can look mysterious from the outside. It's more approachable than it seems — and it's where long time horizons let compounding do its most striking work.

Start here

A pension is essentially a long-term investment account with particular tax advantages and rules about when the money can be accessed. A pension is not itself an investment — investments sit inside it.

Many workplace pensions begin when someone is relatively young and stay invested for several decades. That long horizon is exactly the setting in which compounding matters most — which is why a mature pension pot is often far more than the sum of the money paid into it.
