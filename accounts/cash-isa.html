Cash ISA

An ISA that holds cash rather than investments — and a clean way to see the saving-vs-investing line.

What a Cash ISA is

A Cash ISA is an ISA that holds cash. Instead of investments that rise and fall, it earns interest, much like an ordinary savings account — but with ISA tax treatment.

  • Interest. Your money earns a rate of interest rather than investment returns.
  • Tax treatment. Interest earned within a Cash ISA is generally sheltered from UK tax.
  • Access. Depending on the specific product, access may be instant or fixed for a term.
  • Allowance. Paying into a Cash ISA uses part of the same overall ISA allowance (£20,000 across all your ISAs).

How it differs from a Stocks & Shares ISA

Cash ISA

Holds cash. Earns interest. Greater certainty about the number of pounds. This is saving.

Stocks & Shares ISA

Holds investments. Value can rise and fall. Potential for greater long-term returns, with risk of loss. This is investing.

Same wrapper family, very different contents. A Cash ISA is a form of saving; a Stocks & Shares ISA is a form of investing. The “ISA” label alone doesn't tell you which.

This makes the Cash ISA a helpful reminder of the site's core distinction. Cash offers certainty over the amount held but, like all cash, it's exposed to inflation over long periods. Which is appropriate depends on what the money is for and when you'll need it — the theme of saving vs investing.

A quick way to see it

If a Cash ISA pays 3% interest while prices rise by 3%, the number of pounds grows but their purchasing power stays roughly flat. If prices rise faster than the interest rate, purchasing power can fall even though the balance goes up. That's not an argument against cash — it's just why cash tends to suit shorter-term money rather than decades-long goals.

Not advice

Interest rates, tax rules and product terms vary and change. This is general information, not a recommendation to choose any particular account.
