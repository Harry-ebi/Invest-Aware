Funds

The single most practical tool for owning lots of investments without buying each one yourself.

What it isHow it worksWhy it matters

Pooled investment, in one picture

A fund pools money from many investors and uses it to buy a wide range of underlying investments. When you put money into a fund, you own a share of that whole collection — not the individual holdings directly.

Why funds matter

Buying hundreds of individual shares or bonds yourself would be expensive, time-consuming and impractical for most people. A fund does the spreading for you, which is why funds are the usual route to diversification. With a single purchase, your money can be exposed to a broad range of investments at once.

Funds let investors gain exposure to many investments without having to buy each one individually — the everyday way most people own “the haystack”.

One purchase of a broad fund can mean holding a small piece of many investments at once — compare that with the single highlighted slice on the shares page.

Funds come in many forms. Some are actively managed, aiming to beat a market; others are passive, aiming to track one. Some are bought and sold directly with the fund provider; others, called ETFs, trade on an exchange. What they share is the pooling principle above.

Worth remembering

A fund reduces reliance on any single investment, but it doesn't remove market risk — if the whole market falls, a broad fund generally falls too. Funds also carry charges, which vary and are worth understanding.
